{% else %}

Where traceroutes go

Latest traceroute per probe. Each spoke is one probe; it starts at the outside and steps inward one hop at a time toward the target in the middle. Dots are colored by network. A red ✕ marks where a path stopped. Click a network in the legend to highlight it.

Carrier scorecard

One row per group. Reached = share of traceroutes that got to the target in this window. Latency is the round-trip time of pings from the group's probes (median and 90th percentile). Loss = ping packets that didn't come back. Click a row to highlight its probes in the map above.

Latency & loss by group

Line = median round-trip time across the group's probes; band = spread between the group's fast and slow probes (10th–90th percentile once a group has 5 or more); red bars = packet loss (right axis). Same time scale on every chart, so a problem that only affects one carrier stands out.

Where latency is added

Median jump in round-trip time when a path crosses from one network into the next (latest traceroutes, at least 2 probes). Large jumps are usually distance (an ocean); a jump that's new or uneven between carriers points at congestion.

Where failed traceroutes stop

For probes whose latest traceroute didn't reach the target: the last network that answered. Many stops in one network suggest that's where traffic toward you is being dropped.
