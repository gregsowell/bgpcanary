How the paths are found: RIS doesn't show the paths between two networks directly. It shows each RIS peer's AS path toward your prefix and toward theirs, and every network on such a path has a route of its own to that destination. Your traffic's paths go up from your AS through the networks that carry your prefix, and through your upstreams' other neighbours (from RIPEstat, as on the connectivity tree), to the first network with a route of its own to theirs, then along that route. Their traffic's paths are their own route to you when RIS sees it, or the same climb from their side. A network sends traffic up to its providers for anywhere, but across to a peer only for that peer's own customers, so the climb goes on only through providers (your transits, and the networks in front of each one on many paths), and a peer's route counts only when it stays among that peer's customers. Your exchange peers and listed peers (Settings › Monitored network) are peers. RIS rarely sees a peering, so where PeeringDB has you, or one of your transits, at an exchange with them, that link is shown dotted as a possible path. So these are the paths the data supports, not every path that exists. The traceroute from this server shows which one your traffic takes; a RIPE Atlas traceroute from a probe in their network (or their main upstream) shows theirs. Their side comes from RIPEstat's looking glass (and Route Views, if asked), yours from bgpcanary's live view. Traceroute hops are mapped to networks with Team Cymru's IP-to-ASN service, and hops at internet exchanges with PeeringDB.
